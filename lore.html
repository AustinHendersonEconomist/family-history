Lore vs. records

Family stories and family trees, set beside the documents. Each entry links to what it rests on, so anyone can check it and disagree.

FamilySearch records need a free account. Most were read from FamilySearch’s index, which is typed from the handwriting and has mistakes. The image is the final word where there is one.

The stories

No record found

An ancestor was a Texas Ranger

The Texas State Library’s index of Ranger and related service records has no Thomas B., Robert P. or William C. Henderson. The index is incomplete, so this weakens the story without killing it. What the records do show is Thomas B. Henderson in Gould’s Battalion of Texas Cavalry, a Confederate unit raised in Houston County in 1862. Mounted Texans were often remembered as “rangers”.

The family was connected with Davy Crockett

Nobody on the direct Henderson line was in Texas before about 1847, eleven years after the Alamo. One son was born in Mississippi in 1847 and the next in Texas in 1849. But they then spent thirty years around Crockett, Texas, named for him.

1850 census1870 censusHandbook of Texas: Crockett
Unproven

The Crockett story is really Daniel Boone, misremembered

Possible, but nothing proves it. Boone touches the family in three places. Richard Henderson, the judge who hired Boone to open Kentucky, shares the surname but has no known link. The Hendersons lived in Lexington, Kentucky, in Boone country, in the late 1950s. And Capt. James Ward of Kentucky, brother of an ancestor on the Snouse and Ashmead side, is said to have been a pallbearer when Boone was reburied in 1845. That last tie is on the other side of the family from the Crockett story.

They were among the first settlers of Austin

Austin was founded in 1839. No Henderson ancestor was in Texas by then, and the censuses place them in Houston, Coryell, Coleman and McLennan counties. Thomas B.’s second wife, Lavenia, was born in Texas about 1833, so her family could be where this began. She isn’t an ancestor.

The 1850, 1870 and 1880 censuses say Georgia, 1860 says Tennessee, and his death record says Mississippi. His father’s 1830 will was written in Elbert County, Georgia, so Georgia is the best answer.
