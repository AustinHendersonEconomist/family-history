Across the Atlantic

How far back the European lines really go, and where the parish registers run out.

The shared tree on FamilySearch lists about 8,900 ancestors in these branches, most of them in Europe and many of them medieval. Very little of that is proven. For this page, European-born ancestors within about twelve generations, with a full birth date from about 1580 on, were looked up in FamilySearch’s parish indexes: 398 people. 62 matched a baptism or christening of the right name at about the right date, usually with a parent who fits. A few of those are clearly the wrong child. The rest matched nothing, or only a gravestone or memorial page.

A match shows that someone of that name was baptised there. It doesn’t show that this child is the person who emigrated. Lines where several generations match in the same parish are much stronger than a single hit.

The strongest lines

Good so far

Struthers, of East Kilbride and Sorn

Four generations with baptisms, in two neighbouring counties. John, baptised 1690, son of Andrew. James, 1715, son of John. James, 1736, son of James. Then Ann, baptised at Sorn in 1802, daughter of James Struthers and Mary Watson. She married John McMaster and died in Illinois. The gap is Ann’s father James (born 1769 in the tree), whose own baptism wasn’t found, and the 1841 census supports Ann’s end of it. The family were Lowland Scots all the way.

Broucard (Brokaw), Walloons of Mannheim

A new find, through Effie McClain’s grandmother Lucinda Betterton. Jeanne Broucard was baptised on 17 November 1667 in the French-speaking refugee church at Mannheim, daughter of Bourgon Broucard and Catherine. The family went on to Long Island and New Jersey, where they became the Brokaws. A published study covers their origins. The steps down to Lucinda, through the Covert and Bird families of New Jersey and Virginia, are still from the tree.

Praa, Huguenots of Leiden

Also through Lucinda Betterton. Anne Prat was baptised at Leiden on 4 February 1652, daughter of Pierre Prat and Marie Philippe. The tree gives her birth in Brabant and makes her the Annetje Praa of Brooklyn. Brooklyn’s Praa family is well known, but the link to this baptism should be checked.

Eisele and Meyer, of Köngen, Württemberg

Behind Jacob Yeazel. Köngen and Wendlingen, side by side on the Neckar, give baptisms for Anna Maria Meyer (1689), Anna Klee (1654), Ursula Rempiss (1631) and David Rempiss (1597). But the tree moves the family to Wies in Baden for one generation and gives Jacob’s father a lifespan of 112 years, so the join to Jacob is weak.

Snouse (Schnaus), of Saxony

The tree has no parents for Michael Schnaus and no village. His gravestone gives 6 January 1797, and the 1860 census makes him two years older. Without a village the registers can’t be searched. John’s Civil War pension file or a Greenville church book may name it.

Royal and noble descents

The tree runs from the Ashmeads, Millers and Bishops to the Nevilles, the Beauforts and beyond. Some may be real, but each depends on unsourced steps in the 1600s.
