Worth checking

Ashmead: one Ancestry tree makes Samuel Ashmead (born 1794 in Maryland) the son of Capt. John Ashmead and Mary Mifflin of Philadelphia. Mary would have been 47, and FamilySearch doesn’t make the link, so the map shows it as doubtful.

Gertrude Smith: an earlier version of this map said she was born in Iowa. That was wrong. The 1910 and 1920 censuses say Illinois, and her daughter’s 1933 birth record says Galesburg, as the Ancestry tree does.

Huber: FamilySearch places Christina Huber’s birth in “Mecklenburg”, but the 1860 census confirms Württemberg.

McMaster: Mary M. Thompson’s parents turned out to be William A. Thompson and Mary E. McMaster of Altona, Illinois (1904 marriage record).

A Mohawk ancestor: through Lorinda Multer’s mother, the tree reaches Jacques Van Slyck of Schenectady, whose mother was a Mohawk woman. Local histories document that, though not her name. She would be a direct ancestor. Every link down to Lorinda is backed by a church record or published genealogy, except one, Santje Vrooman’s parentage, which is probable.

About this map

Lore vs. records lists where the documents and the stories disagree.

This map was researched by Austin in November 2026, with Claude (an AI model), using census and vital records on FamilySearch, an Ancestry family tree, Dutch Reformed church records from Schoharie, Pearson’s genealogies of the first settlers of Schenectady, a Salt Lake County death record, published county histories, and sourced profiles on WikiTree for the earliest families. Some links in those early lines still rest on family trees.

Thick lines are journeys backed by a record. Thin lines come from family trees that haven't been checked, dashed lines are reasonable guesses (for example, an emigration date worked out from where children were born), and dotted lines are family stories that remain unproven.

★ stories set the family against the history happening around them. Turn off “Pause for ★ stories” to let the map run straight through.
