School and work

Classrooms, colleges, farms, a tailor’s shop and a NASA laboratory, from printed biographies, school reports and research papers.

Thomas W. Snouse, physicist

Grandson of an Iowa-born insurance man, raised on a Knox County farm, at Stanford by 1953 and at NASA’s Ames Research Center within a decade. His papers are about sputtering, which is what happens when fast particles knock atoms off a surface, and about whether particles from the Sun could make the Moon glow. They were published while NASA was working out what the lunar surface was made of.

1861–65

John Snouse, 44th Ohio Infantry

Now confirmed. An 1883 Iowa county history says the Nashua tailor served two and a half years in Company G, was badly wounded in Kentucky and still suffered from it. It also says he went to school and learned his trade in Ohio, which is probably why later records make him Ohio-born.

Thompsons and McMasters of Altona, Illinois

The 1912 History of Knox County has sketches of both families. It fills a gap: the Thompson line had never been traced past David.

1835–94

David Thompson

Born at Elkhart, Indiana, 6 December 1835, son of John Thompson and Catherine Livingston, early settlers of Altona. District school, then farming. Three years in Company G, 89th Illinois Infantry, much of it on the forage wagon. Four brothers enlisted too: one was killed and one wounded.

Was there a Herbert Hoover connection?

Family memory suggests Thomas’s going to Stanford had something to do with Hoover. No connection has turned up. That isn’t the same as there being none.

  • Not by blood, as far as the tree shows. About 8,900 ancestors on both sides were compared with 2,001 of Hoover’s and 1,656 of Lou Henry Hoover’s. Nobody is shared later than about 1549. The few shared names before that (a Welsh couple, a Swiss Huber, some English gentry) rest on unsourced trees.
  • “Huber” is a coincidence so far. Hoover’s name was Huber, and Christina Snouse was a Huber. But his Hubers came from the Palatinate and hers from Württemberg, and no link between them is known. (Hoover’s immigrant ancestor; Christina, 1860.)
  • A marriage link can’t be ruled out. There are too many in-laws to check without a name. Hoover’s close families were Minthorn, Davis, Yount, Coate, Wasley, Toole and Sherwood. If any of those rings a bell, it can be tested quickly.
  • Other Iowa and Stanford threads. Harry Snouse and Hoover were both born in Iowa in 1874. Stanford’s first president, David Starr Jordan, taught at Lombard in Galesburg in the early 1870s, and Helen Snouse graduated from Lombard. A “J. W. Hoover” worked on the same 1918 Galesburg bond drive as Harry. None of these is evidence of anything yet. (Jordan; 1918 list.)

What would settle it: a Stanford admission or scholarship letter, his student file from the Stanford registrar (which family can ask for), or the correspondence files at the Hoover Institution and the Hoover Presidential Library.

Full-text records are machine transcriptions on FamilySearch, and a free account is needed. Galesburg yearbooks and newspapers haven’t been searched.
